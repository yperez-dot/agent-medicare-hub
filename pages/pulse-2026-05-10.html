The Week in 5

🚨
Auto-enrollment into Medicare Advantage gains momentum, drawing sharp pushback
The Trump administration's CMS is actively considering making Medicare Advantage the default enrollment for newly eligible beneficiaries — a Project 2025 plank that Medicare director Chris Klomp has confirmed is under feasibility review. This week brought two prominent op-eds (Fortune May 7, Forbes May 9) arguing the policy would lock seniors into prior-auth-heavy plans they didn't choose. For THEI: if this advances, the AEP sales motion changes fundamentally — newly eligible 65-year-olds in Florida would arrive already enrolled rather than shopping. Watch for a formal proposed rule or RFI; nothing has been published yet.

📋
Medicare Advantage Improvement Act of 2026 (HR 8375 / S 4384) is live in Congress
Bipartisan bill introduced in late April, gaining traction this week, would require MA plans to follow traditional Medicare's medical necessity standards, mandate real-time approvals for routine/low-risk services, prohibit algorithmic auto-denials, and add a "Compliance and Coverage Protection" domain to MA Star Ratings. Companion bill from Reps. Jayapal/Khanna (Stop Deadly Denials Act) goes further by banning prior auth in MA outright. For THEI: if any version passes, prior-auth-driven plan dissatisfaction — a top reason members switch at AEP — could ease. Star ratings would also become harder to game on the consumer-protection side. Track committee markup over the next 30–60 days.

🌴
MA network contractions hitting Florida seniors as hospitals exit plans
Yahoo Finance (May 2026) and Modern Healthcare reporting confirm the trend: the two largest MA insurers, UHC and Humana, will offer plans in about 80% of US counties in 2026 — down from nearly 90% in 2025 — and provider-network terminations (Johns Hopkins/UHC, Scripps medical groups, others) keep stacking up. Florida's dense MA market and concentrated senior population mean THEI clients are disproportionately exposed to mid-year network changes and AEP plan exits. For THEI: build a proactive outreach list of clients on plans most at risk of network shrinkage, especially anyone tied to a specific hospital system.

📰
UnitedHealth and Humana Q1 2026 earnings beat; both reaffirm 2026 outlook
UnitedHealth posted $7.23 adjusted EPS (vs. $6.61 estimate), medical cost ratio improved 90 bps to 83.9%; stock surged 37% in April. Humana reported $10.31 adjusted EPS (vs. $10.19 estimate) and projects ~25% MA membership growth versus 2025, though it flagged Star Ratings as a likely 2027 headwind. Combined with CMS's 2.48% CY2027 rate increase (released April 6), carriers have financial cushion to keep benefits relatively stable through this AEP. For THEI: don't expect aggressive benefit cuts from these two at AEP 2027 product release — but Humana's Star Ratings warning is worth tracking because it could affect quality bonus payments and, indirectly, supplemental benefit budgets.

KFF: 53 million prior authorization requests submitted to MA plans in 2024, 7.7% denied
New KFF data this week quantifies what agents have been seeing operationally: prior auth volume in MA continues to climb, with about 4.1 million denials in 2024. KFF also confirms more enrollees are now in MA-PDs than standalone PDPs (Part D enrollment hit 56 million in February 2026). For THEI: this is the data that fuels the MAIA and Stop Deadly Denials bills above — and it's useful framing when explaining to clients why prior auth experiences vary so much by carrier and plan. Not for marketing materials (CMS marketing rules), but useful for internal training and consultative conversations.
