Medicare GLP-1 Bridge is live — $50/month for eligible Part D enrollees as of July 1

4-minute read

CMS launched the Medicare GLP-1 Bridge on July 1, 2026 — a demonstration program giving eligible Medicare Part D enrollees access to three GLP-1 weight-loss medications at a flat $50/month copayment: Wegovy® (injection or tablet), Zepbound® (KwikPen only — not single-dose vials or pens), and Foundayo® (tablet). The $50 payment does not count toward the Part D deductible or annual out-of-pocket cap, and Low-Income Subsidy (LIS/Extra Help) cost-sharing does not apply. CMS — not the plan — manages prior authorization, claims adjudication, and pharmacy payments through a central processor; Part D sponsors don't need to opt in and don't carry risk for these drugs. The program runs through December 31, 2027. KFF estimates nearly 4 million Medicare beneficiaries could meet the eligibility criteria. The planned follow-on program (the BALANCE Model) has been delayed indefinitely, making the Bridge the operative coverage vehicle through the end of next year.

What this means for you: Client calls are coming — here's your talking points. Eligible beneficiaries must: (1) have Part D coverage, (2) have a BMI ≥30 or ≥27 with a qualifying weight-related comorbidity, (3) not have previously received a GLP-1 under Part D for weight loss, and (4) get prior authorization through CMS (not their plan). Clients using GLP-1s for type 2 diabetes or cardiovascular risk management continue to access those through their standard Part D benefit — the Bridge is only for weight-management indications. Scan your Part D-enrolled book for clients who have asked about weight-loss drugs; this is a high-value retention touchpoint. Direct eligible clients to Medicare.gov/coverage/weight-loss-drugs for the eligibility wizard and to call 1-800-MEDICARE for prior auth questions.

Source: CMS / Medicare Rights Center / KFF

📋  IMPORTANT · LEGAL

Elevance sues CMS for $115M over star ratings inequality — enrollment suspension remains in force

3-minute read

Two separate Elevance Health issues are active simultaneously. First: Elevance filed a lawsuit July 1 in Georgia federal court alleging CMS violated the Administrative Procedure Act by giving Clover Health a more favorable 2026 MA star ratings recalculation — excluding 20 measures a court found legally improper — while refusing to apply the same methodology to Elevance's plans. At stake: $115 million in lost 2027 quality bonus payments across five Elevance contracts. CMS recalculated stars for select MA insurers in a June 17 memo but dropped additional measures that Elevance performed well on — a distinction Elevance is now litigating. Second: Elevance's enrollment suspension, imposed March 31 for repeated failures to submit risk-adjustment data through required CMS electronic systems, remains in force as of July 13. CMS has issued no public statement lifting it. Current Elevance/Anthem members are unaffected, but no new enrollments are permitted. 2026 marks the third consecutive year CMS has been forced to recalculate MA star ratings following legal challenges; over $13 billion in bonus payments industry-wide are caught in the litigation wave.

What this means for you: On the enrollment suspension: continue redirecting any Elevance/Anthem MA prospects to other carriers — the suspension is still active and there is no CMS signal of restoration. On the star ratings lawsuit: this is a financial dispute that does not affect current Elevance members and will not trigger a Special Enrollment Period. Track the Georgia court's docket; a preliminary injunction ruling — if granted — would force CMS to recalculate, which could reshape 2027 bonus payments for multiple carriers beyond Elevance. Also watch for UHC's 2027 certification portal to open this month.

Source: Healthcare Finance News / Healthcare Dive / STAT

📋  IMPORTANT · POLICY

OIG: MA plans deny 65% of LTCH prior auth requests; House subcommittee advances H.R. 3514

3-minute read

KFF published a July 6 analysis of two HHS Office of Inspector General reports (released June 11) documenting extraordinary prior authorization denial rates in Medicare Advantage for post-acute care: 65% of long-term care hospital (LTCH) requests denied, 54% of inpatient rehabilitation (IRF) requests denied, and 12% of skilled nursing facility (SNF) requests denied. The most striking finding: 95% of SNF denials were overturned on appeal — confirming the care was medically necessary when initially denied. UHC's NaviHealth subsidiary processed over one-third of all LTCH and IRF prior auth requests and denied them at above-average rates; AHIP pushed back, citing documentation errors and post-acute overutilization as drivers. Separately, the House Energy & Commerce Subcommittee on Health advanced H.R. 3514 (Improving Seniors' Timely Access to Care Act) on June 30, 2026 — bipartisan legislation that would codify 2024 CMS prior auth rules into law and require MA plans to publicly report denial rates by item and service, enabling plan-to-plan comparisons for the first time.

What this means for you: This data is the factual backbone behind frustrated client calls about denied SNF or rehab coverage. You cannot cite OIG reports in marketing materials (CMS rules), but in consultative conversations about plan selection — especially for clients with chronic conditions, recent hospitalizations, or family members in post-acute settings — carrier-level prior auth denial variation is a legitimate planning factor. If H.R. 3514 passes, annual denial data becomes publicly comparable: expect prior auth performance to become a differentiator in plan conversations at AEP. Track committee floor votes; the bill has bipartisan momentum and the OIG data is fresh political fuel.

Source: KFF / OIG HHS / Healthcare Dive / ASNC

UnitedHealth Q1 beat signals MA cost stabilization; Humana Q2 earnings due July 29

2-minute read

UnitedHealth's Q1 2026 results beat estimates ($7.23 adjusted EPS vs. $6.61 expected), with medical cost ratio improving 90 basis points to 83.9% and full-year EPS guidance raised above $18.25. The numbers — reported earlier this spring — sent UNH stock up 9% and lifted Humana 5%, as investors read them as early evidence that the MA repricing cycle is gaining traction across the sector. Humana's Q2 2026 earnings are due July 29 — a more revealing test: Humana added approximately 1 million individual MA members in 2026, and the market is watching whether medical costs on that expanded book are tracking to projections. Humana's Q2 call will also be the first public look at whether the Moffitt Cancer Center network exit (effective July 1) generated any member service volume spikes or continuity-of-care activation costs. Humana's 2026 EPS forecast remains at approximately $9 — well below pre-2024 levels — but Q2 results will determine whether the recovery trajectory is on track for the projected 2027 rebound.

What this means for you: Carrier financial health directly affects network breadth, AEP benefit design, and commission stability heading into 2027. Humana's July 29 call is the one to watch — if medical costs surprise to the upside, expect signals of further benefit tightening or network adjustments at AEP 2027 plan design time. If costs track as guided, Humana's Florida commitment looks stable. The call transcript and investor presentation will be available at humana.com/investors by 6 AM ET on July 29.
